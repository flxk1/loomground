Card 1 of 7. Headline: Governance should not fragment on the demand side. Text: agent capabilities scale on the supply side; add a second agent from a second provider and your rules are written twice, in two formats. Limit: you can run the same agent everywhere; your rules cannot follow it easily.

Card 2 of 7. Headline: One rule, written six ways. List: policy documents, system prompts, workflow code, access settings, model safeguards, approval steps. Limit: each can be checked on its own; none can be checked against another.

Card 3 of 7. Headline: The task travels. The rule stays where it was written. A chain: you, agent A, agent B, tool. Text: the task 'Negotiate this contract. Never accept unlimited liability. Ask me before you say yes.' Agent A hands the negotiation to agent B; B receives the contract, the task, perhaps the authority; nothing carries the two rules from A to B. Limit: API, MCP and A2A carry the task and the data; how to carry the rule easily?

Card 4 of 7. Headline: An agent cannot hold more authority than its principal. Three boxes: principal, level 2; agent acting for it, asks level 4; result, refused. Definition: level is how much an agent may do without asking a person. Limit: this is why the principal gets to set the rules. Source: test case reject-delegation-grade-amplify.

Card 5 of 7. Headline: One line says who decides, and when. Gloss: sending data out at high risk is held for human review. The line: reserve data_transfer by human_review when risk >= high. Stamp: HELD FOR HUMAN REVIEW. Loomground is the small open language the line is written in; a reviewer reads it as a rule, a program runs it as a decision and keeps a record. Limit: it says who decides; it does not judge whether the risk is high or whether the rule is wise.

Card 6 of 7. Headline: Write the rule once. Every agent reads it the same way. Today: provider A, its settings; provider B, its settings. Instead: you, your rules, A, B, C. The rules stay when you change the provider; they go with a task when it is handed on. Limit: a rule between two parties cannot belong to one of them, so the language is open.

Card 7 of 7. Headline: Open source. Install it, test it, get in touch. Text: a small side project; the specification, the test cases and two implementations are on GitHub, free to use. Links: github.com/flxk1/loomground; whitepaper: github.com/flxk1/thoughts.
